### Description

A 24-hour on-campus game jam co-hosted by Unnatural Intelligence and the Game Development Club. One secret theme is revealed at kickoff. Teams of 2–5 have one day to ship a playable prototype, using a **shared club game-dev AI workspace** for art, audio, 3D, and (where the engine allows) assisted implementation, then finishing the game as humans.

This is a **Learn + live-lab** event under Unnatural Intelligence’s foundations: find uses default chat never gets asked (game production), fold AI into a real workflow (a jam, not a demo), and keep a human finish. The Game Development Club covers engines, scoping, and playable-game craft so the AI does not become the whole product.

The event is **not a sleepover**. Jammers may leave overnight and return. Anyone who stays after facility hours is working in a booked room, registered with Campus Security, with at least one named organizer on site at all times. No alcohol.

## 3. Objectives

1. **Ship a playable prototype in 24 hours** — every team leaves with a build or a recorded playthrough, even if the scope is tiny.
2. **Hands-on AI-in-production** — every attendee who wants it gets access to the shared game-dev AI workspace (art / 3D / audio / assisted implementation), with a team credit budget so 30–100 people are not buying individual seats.
3. **Cross-club recruitment** — Unnatural Intelligence and the Game Development Club both run sign-up tables; beginners are paired with people who have shipped something before.
4. **Foundations in public** — *untapped* (AI for games, not chat), *workflow* (a real jam pipeline), *human finish* (AI is a tool; the game still has to play). Feeds later events (workshops, Alberta Game Jam teams, agent labs).

## 5. Logistics, risk & compliance

- Room booking coordinated exclusively by the SECC — no direct booking by the club.
- **Risk level: medium.** On campus, indoor, no alcohol, no physical sport — but **after-hours**, fatigue, food, and dense laptop load. Not a “low-risk two-hour workshop.”
- After-hours access coordinated with SECC and Campus Security (403-284-8530 / campus.security@sait.ca). Occupants remaining after facility hours are listed for Security. This is a booked work session, not an unsanctioned sleepover.
- Quiet hours 01:00–06:00. Jammers may leave and return. No camping furniture unless SECC explicitly allows it.
- If refreshments are served: food and beverages ordered through SAIT’s approved catering vendor only, arranged via the SECC. 72-hour Everyday lead (or 48-hour Express for ≤20).
- Dietary needs collected on RSVP (vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, allergen). Flagged to Curated Catering on the order.
- A/V picked up from SECC and returned after Sunday teardown.
- Primary Event Organizer is responsible for the event. Because 24 hours exceeds one person’s safe awake window, a **written shift roster** keeps a named Unnatural Intelligence or Game Development Club exec on site at every hour. PEO or deputy is always present.
- Campus Security is the first-aid / emergency call. Execs know the Q100 / Eugene Coste security desk and 403-284-8000 emergency number.
- Attendance list signed by every attendee (mandatory for grant-funded events).
- Posters: tabloid 11×17, Saitsa Clubs logo unaltered, submitted via saitsa.com/Poster-Submission before posting. Built-in budget $10. Removed within 48 hours after the event. Do not use the official SAIT logo.
- If SECC classifies this as a **social event**, grant funds must already be in the club account before the event proposal is filed. If classified as an educational live-lab / workshop, follow the two-week (or one-month if ≥50) lead time. Either way: **no spend before grant approval.**
- Events should land one to three months after the grant intake. Target the **5 October 2026** intake for a mid-November date (confirm 2026/27 disbursement table with SECC). Expected attendance over 50 uses the longer planning window.
- Inform the SECC of any external monetary sponsorship. Unused grant funds are reclaimed after close-out.
